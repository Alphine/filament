# Two-stage solar filament instance segmentation, and what limits it

*Technical report draft for the Solar Filament Segmentation Challenge 2026 (IEEE BigData Cup). Target: 4 pages in the organizers' Overleaf template; this file is the content, to be typeset there.*

## Abstract
We segment solar filaments in H-alpha images with a two-stage pipeline: a five-fold ResNet34 U-Net ensemble with dihedral test-time augmentation proposes candidate instances, and a crop-level refiner redraws each candidate from a box-shaped seed. On a 50-image hold-out never used for training or model selection the pipeline reaches PQ 0.4325 (bootstrap SE 0.017) and 0.36 on the public leaderboard. Two analyses shape our interpretation. First, two annotators disagree with each other at PQ 0.34 and mean matched IoU 0.63, so a large share of the remaining error is annotation ambiguity rather than model error. Second, most post-processing and loss ideas we tried (watershed, shape filters, topology losses, TTA gating, ensembling with a public model) did not help at the resolution our hold-out can measure. Candidate experiments that would need new GPU training (a full clDice loss, a vesselness input channel, a second ensemble member, self-supervised pretraining on public H-alpha images) were audited analytically and not run, because their expected gains (about +-0.005 or less) are below what a 50-image hold-out can resolve. We also document that the public leaderboard's upper range is not consistent with legitimate modelling, and how we complied with the competition's data rules.

## 1. Data and evaluation protocol
**Data.** 707 annotated training images (2048x2048, MAGFiLO [9]) and 180 test images. The full MAGFiLO collection has 10,244 annotated filaments from 1,593 GONG observations and 41 contributing annotators [9]. 296 training images have two or more annotator passes (145 with two, 151 with three); 411 have one.

**Metric.** PQ [1] = sum(IoU of TP pairs) / (|TP| + 0.5|FP| + 0.5|FN|), TP if IoU > 0.5, evaluated against each annotator pass separately and pooled (`filament_metric.py`; verified equal to our earlier implementation on randomized data, identical TP/FP/FN counts).

**Hold-out.** 50 images chosen with a fixed seed (1234), excluded from every training fold, from checkpoint selection and from the refiner's training. All model comparisons below are on this set. Its resolution is limited: a per-image bootstrap gives PQ 0.4325 +- 0.017 (95% CI 0.399-0.465), so unpaired differences below roughly 0.03 cannot be distinguished from noise. We therefore report per-image results (`analysis/results/fpdiag`) and treat gains under about 0.005 as unproven.

## 2. Method
**Stage-A.** Five U-Nets [2] with ImageNet-initialised ResNet34 [3] encoders, one per fold of the 657 non-hold-out images (about 526 train / 131 checkpoint-selection images per fold; 30 epochs of 220 steps x batch 8 of 512-px patches, 75% of them centred on filaments; loss = Tversky [4] with alpha 0.3, beta 0.7 (penalises missed pixels more) + BCE; AdamW [5], one-cycle LR [6] peaking at 1e-4). Targets are the pixel union of all annotator passes of an image. Inference averages 5 models x 4 flips (identity, left-right, up-down, 180 deg) over 512-px windows with stride 384.

**Candidates.** `prob > 0.4`, 3x3 closing, 8-connected components; keep area 400-40000 px and mean probability >= 0.7. These thresholds were tuned on the hold-out; a later simulation of tightening them found no out-of-sample gain (split-half cross-validation mean -0.0005), i.e. they sit near a local optimum.

**Crop refiner.** For each candidate a square crop (1.5x the longest bounding-box side, min 96 px) is resized to 512x512 and fed as `[crop, CLAHE(crop) [7], seed]`, where the seed is the bounding box padded by 40% (during training the pad is drawn from 20-60%). A `smp.Unet(resnet34)` [8] predicts the mask (threshold 0.5). Training uses every annotator instance as one sample (6,474 crops) with the seed box jittered (shift +-15%, scale 0.85-1.25) so the network cannot memorise the exact box; loss 0.45 BCE + 0.55 soft Dice, 13 epochs. Using the real mask as seed at training time makes the refiner copy its input and fail at test time.

**Near-limb filter (added 27 Sep 2026).** The solar disk is estimated per image (Otsu threshold, largest component, holes filled, radius from its area), and every final instance whose centroid lies beyond 0.88 of the disk radius is dropped (`filament_pipeline.drop_near_limb`, on by default; `Pipeline(limb_drop=None)` restores the earlier pipeline). The threshold was chosen out of fold on 657 images (+0.0065 PQ), and the hold-out rises from 0.4325 to 0.4384 (section 3).

**Score distributions and fragmentation.** Beyond the pooled PQ, we score the 400 true-positive pairs on the hold-out individually. IoU: mean 0.676, median 0.686 (p5 0.531, p25 0.609, p75 0.740, p95 0.805). Dice: mean 0.803, median 0.813 (p5 0.694, p25 0.757, p75 0.851, p95 0.892) -- computed from `analysis/scripts/filament_distributions_cpu.py`, run once on the full pipeline against the official hold-out (integrity check: reproduces PQ 0.43246, TP/FP/FN 400/239/211 exactly). We also check for one-to-many and many-to-one relations, using a lower "meaningful overlap" threshold of IoU>0.1 (separate from the official IoU>0.5 match rule) to detect predictions/ground-truth instances that partially overlap more than one counterpart. Of 609 (ground-truth instance, annotator pass) pairs on the hold-out, 27 (4.4%) ground-truth filaments are split across two or more predictions (one-to-many, over-segmentation), against 9 of 639 predictions (1.4%) that merge two or more ground-truth filaments into one (many-to-one, under-segmentation/merging); the rest are clean 1:1 matches (64.9% of ground-truth instances), pure misses (17.1%) or one-prediction near-misses below IoU 0.5 (13.6%). The pipeline over-segments about three times more often than it merges, consistent with the candidate step treating touching or crossing filaments conservatively.

**Out-of-fold evaluation (657 images).** To measure future changes with better resolution than the hold-out allows, each of the 657 pool images is scored by the stage-A model whose fold did not train on it (single model, 4-flip TTA, production thresholds, crop refiner; `analysis/scripts/filament_oof_cpu.py`, splits reproduced exactly from the training script). PQ 0.388 (SE 0.0047, CI95 0.379-0.397; TP/FP/FN 4599/3769/2989); without the refiner 0.367 (SE 0.0046), so the refiner adds +0.021, in line with the hold-out's +0.018 (the refiner was trained on these images, so this figure is optimistic, while the without-refiner figure is not). Per fold: 0.394, 0.372, 0.373, 0.396, 0.407. By number of annotator passes: 0.394 (380 single-pass images), 0.392 (136 two-pass), 0.380 (141 three-pass). The standard error is about 3.6 times smaller than the hold-out's, so paired comparisons of stage-A variants on these images can resolve differences of roughly 0.005. The stored probability and label maps also let post-processing be re-scored on 657 images without running the networks again.

**Hold-out optimism versus ensemble gain.** Each fold model alone on the hold-out (same protocol; `analysis/scripts/filament_holdout_single_cpu.py`, `analyze_holdout_single.py`) scores 0.4067 on average with the refiner (0.3855 without), against 0.388 on its own out-of-fold images: a hold-out effect of +0.018 (CI95 -0.017 to +0.050, not significant), and the 5-model ensemble adds +0.026 on the hold-out. An ensemble on out-of-fold-like images would therefore score about 0.41, which still leaves about 0.05 to the leaderboard's 0.36 unexplained by hold-out optimism -- pointing to a difference in the test images.

**Consensus-target pilot (fold 1, GPU).** Two arms trained with the production recipe and the same seed (`training/filament_consensus_pilot.py`): the union target (control) and a consensus target that, on multi-pass images, keeps only instances matched (IoU > 0.3) by an instance of another pass (target pixels -11%). Scored on fold 1's 132 out-of-fold images with the production pipeline, consensus gives 0.4098 against 0.3954 for the union (+0.0144, paired CI95 +0.002 to +0.026); the union re-run differs from the released fold-1 model by only +0.0014, so run-to-run noise is small. The consensus model trades 7% of true positives for 34% fewer false positives (TP/FP/FN 882/540/581 against 948/820/515). The gain appears only with the refiner (without it +0.003, CI includes 0); the refiner was trained on these images, but the hold-out, which it never saw, moves the same way (+0.0155, CI95 -0.011 to +0.040 on 50 images). This passes the decision rule fixed before the run (>= +0.005 with a paired CI excluding 0). Each arm took about 25 minutes of T4 training. Script `analysis/scripts/analyze_pilot.py`, results `analysis/results/pilot/`.

**Consensus target, all five folds: not better (not submitted).** Folds 2-5 were trained with the consensus target the same way and each scored on its own out-of-fold images. Pooled over the 657 images the consensus models score 0.3903 against 0.3879 for the released models (+0.0023, paired CI95 -0.003 to +0.008) and are worse without the refiner (-0.0063, CI95 -0.012 to -0.001). Per fold: +0.016, +0.002, +0.012, +0.003, -0.020; the false-positive reduction seen in the pilot did not hold (false positives rose in folds 3 and 5). The 5-model consensus ensemble on the hold-out scores 0.4174 against 0.4325 for the released ensemble (-0.015, CI95 -0.029 to -0.003). Both submit criteria fixed beforehand failed (out-of-fold gain >= +0.005 with CI above 0; hold-out ensemble not worse), so nothing was submitted. The single-fold pilot's +0.014 was within the fold-to-fold spread (about +-0.015), which is larger than the effect being tested -- a one-fold pilot is not enough evidence with this recipe. Scripts `training/filament_consensus_pilot.py` (FOLDS=2,3 and 4,5), `training/filament_consensus_ensemble.py`, `analysis/scripts/analyze_consensus_full.py`.

**Checkpoint selection: an exponential moving average of the weights beats the production rule.** The production rule keeps the checkpoint with the best PQ on 20 fold-validation images, checked every 5 epochs; in the consensus run the two worst folds had picked epoch 10. One production training run per fold (union target, fixed seed; `training/filament_ckpt_selection.py`) was scored four ways on the fold's validation images minus the 20 the production rule selects on (557 images over five folds; production pipeline with refiner): production rule 0.3855, last epoch 0.3977, SWA of epochs 21-30 0.3971, EMA of the weights (decay 0.999, updated every step) 0.3998. EMA minus production: +0.0144, paired CI95 +0.009 to +0.021, positive in all five folds (+0.004, +0.006, +0.056, +0.005, +0.007; the production rule chose epoch 10 in fold 3), and the fold-to-fold standard deviation drops from 0.025 to 0.007. This passes the rule fixed before the run. Without the refiner there is no gain (EMA -0.0016, CI95 -0.007 to +0.005). The released models were chosen with the production rule, so part of their variance is selection noise. Engineering note: the first attempt failed at epoch 22 because torch 2.10's default SWA average rejects BatchNorm's integer buffers (the local test ran torch 2.13); the rerun uses an explicit average. Resuming from the failed runs' checkpoints did not happen (the outputs of errored kernel versions were apparently not mounted as inputs), so the folds were retrained from scratch. Script `analysis/scripts/analyze_ckpt_selection.py`, results `analysis/results/ckpt_selection/`.

**EMA at the ensemble level: submit gate failed (not submitted).** The 5-model ensemble of the EMA weights, with the released refiner and thresholds, scores 0.4223 on the hold-out against 0.4325 for the released ensemble (-0.010, paired CI95 -0.027 to +0.006; TP/FP/FN 387/240/224 against 400/239/211). The gate fixed beforehand required "not worse", so the validated test predictions (180 images, 1,357 rows) were not submitted. Two readings: averaging five models already removes most of the checkpoint-selection noise that EMA removes for a single model; and this gate favours the released ensemble, since the thresholds and many earlier choices were tuned on this hold-out (estimated hold-out optimism +0.018). A fairer ensemble-level test would compare the EMA ensemble with the production-rule ensemble from the same new training runs (weights `sel_fold*_prod.pt`), both untuned on the hold-out. Script `analysis/scripts/filament_ema_ensemble_cpu.py` (run on a T4), `analysis/scripts/analyze_ema_ensemble.py`.

**EMA versus the production rule at the ensemble level, and how lucky the released ensemble is.** That fairer test (`analysis/scripts/analyze_ema_vs_prod.py`; the EMA hold-out counts were reproduced exactly on CPU for 25 images): the production-rule ensemble of the new runs scores 0.4191 (TP/FP/FN 370/219/241), the EMA ensemble 0.4223, the released ensemble 0.4325. EMA minus production rule at the ensemble level is +0.0032 (CI95 -0.011 to +0.018): no measurable difference -- averaging five models already removes the checkpoint-selection noise that EMA removes for a single model. Re-training the unchanged production recipe gives an ensemble 0.013 below the released one on the hold-out (CI95 -0.030 to +0.002). The released 0.4325 is therefore partly a favourable draw on a hold-out whose thresholds were tuned for exactly those models; together with the single-model estimate of hold-out optimism (+0.018), a realistic hold-out expectation for this recipe is about 0.41-0.42, closer to the leaderboard's 0.36. The released models are kept.

**Leaderboard probe of the EMA ensemble.** Because the hold-out favours the released models, the EMA ensemble's test predictions were submitted once as a probe (clearly labelled as such, not as a replacement; submission 56590617): public leaderboard 0.36, the same as the released pipeline (0.36) and the released+Mask R-CNN ensemble (0.36). On data never used for tuning, the -0.010 hold-out gap does not appear; with two displayed decimals and 180 images the leaderboard cannot resolve differences below about +-0.005-0.02, so EMA and the released ensemble are indistinguishable. The released pipeline stays the described solution.

**The leaderboard gap is not explained by the test images.** On 27 Sep 2026 the public leaderboard had 730 teams: 52 at 0.49-0.62 (the group the organizers consider invalid), then 0.41 (2 teams), 0.40 (11), 0.39 (21), ...; our 0.36 is shared by 67 teams. The expected score of this recipe (out-of-fold single model 0.388 plus ensemble effect about +0.026, i.e. about 0.41) is about 0.05 above its leaderboard score. Checks using only unlabelled test information: predictions per image are similar on the test set and the hold-out (7.5 vs 8.4; ground truth 7.7 per annotator pass); the observatory-site mix is similar; the test set has more 2019-2022 images (14%), but out-of-fold PQ varies little by year or site, and re-weighting the per-year or per-site out-of-fold PQ to the test mix changes it by only -0.002 and -0.000. The remaining candidate is a difference in the test labels themselves (annotators, boundary width, how many small filaments are marked); predicted masks are 14% larger on the test set than on the hold-out (median 1,320 vs 1,158 px), a weak hint in that direction. Only leaderboard feedback can test this.

**Leaderboard probes of post-processing variants (not adopted).** One GPU run of the released pipeline produced five variants from the same stage-A maps (`analysis/scripts/filament_lb_probe_gpu.py`, with a fail-fast check on the first hold-out image and an integrity gate reproducing 400/239/211 before the test set). Hold-out PQ: base 0.4325; strict (mean probability >= 0.8, 28% fewer test instances) 0.4178; loose (p > 0.3, area >= 200, mean >= 0.6, 13% more) 0.4160; masks eroded by ~2 px 0.2698; dilated by ~2 px 0.3191. The +-2 px variants were not submitted (thin filaments lose IoU > 0.5 matches, so they would only confirm a large drop). Strict and loose were submitted as labelled probes: both 0.36, like the released pipeline and the EMA ensemble. The number of predicted instances is therefore not the lever; the leaderboard's two displayed decimals cannot resolve effects below about +-0.01, and no post-processing change we can make reaches that size, so further probing was stopped rather than spending submissions.

**Dropping near-limb instances helps (first post-processing change that passes both out of fold and on the hold-out).** Motivated by the diagnostic that phantom false positives cluster near the solar limb, and by a public write-up that masks the off-disk region. The disk is estimated per image (Otsu threshold, largest component, holes filled; radius R from its area). On the stored out-of-fold label maps (657 images, integrity: base counts equal to the stored ones on 657/657) and the released ensemble's hold-out maps: dropping every instance whose centroid lies beyond 0.90 R gives +0.0059 out of fold (paired CI95 +0.004 to +0.008; TP 4599 -> 4550, FP 3769 -> 3410) and +0.0052 on the hold-out; the selection-aware split-half CV gain over the 10 rules tried is +0.0058 (positive in 100% of 500 splits). Clipping the masks at the same radius instead hurts (-0.0082). Only 4.9% of predicted instances lie beyond 0.90 R. The gain still rose at the edge of the grid, so a wider grid (0.75-0.90) was scored next. Scripts `analysis/scripts/filament_limb_mask_cpu.py`, `analyze_limb_mask.py`.

Wider grid (`filament_limb_drop_ext_cpu.py`, `analyze_limb_drop_ext.py`; rule fixed beforehand: t = argmax out-of-fold PQ, submit if its out-of-fold CI excludes 0 and its hold-out effect is >= 0): out of fold 0.75 -0.028, 0.80 -0.011, 0.85 +0.002, 0.88 +0.0065 (CI95 +0.004 to +0.009), 0.90 +0.0059; hold-out 0.85 +0.007, 0.88 +0.0059 (0.4325 -> 0.4384), 0.90 +0.0052; selection-aware CV gain +0.0061. Tighter cut-offs remove real filaments near the limb. drop@0.88 was applied to the released pipeline's test predictions (44 of 1,347 instances removed) and submitted (submission 56600538): public leaderboard 0.36 -- a gain of this size is below the two displayed decimals. It is the only post-processing change in this study that is positive both out of fold and on the hold-out.

**Crop refiner generation 2: no gain (refiner saturated).** Because 54% of the errors are near-misses and the refiner was the one component that ever helped, three refiner recipes were compared cross-fitted: the pool was split into two halves (fixed seed), refiners were trained on one half and applied to the stored out-of-fold stage-A candidates of the other (`training/filament_refiner_gen2.py`, which imports the production refiner code; fail-fast check: the released refiner reproduced the stored out-of-fold counts). On the 329 images of the first half: production recipe 0.3919, Lovasz-hinge loss 0.3919, EfficientNet-B3 encoder 0.3909; 4-flip test-time augmentation of the crop adds about +0.0014 to each. The second half was cancelled before it used GPU time, because it would have had to show about +0.010 for any arm to reach the pre-set +0.005 bar (futility stop, decided after seeing the first half). The refiner's loss and capacity are not what limits boundary quality; the near-misses are more plausibly the boundary ambiguity between annotators (pairwise annotator PQ 0.34). Results `analysis/results/refiner_gen2/`.

**Spine multi-task supervision for stage-A: better raw detection, absorbed by the refiner (not adopted).** Every training annotation carries a manually drawn spine (checked: 100% of 8,199 annotations, x-y order verified by 99.6% of rasterised spine pixels falling inside the mask). Stage-A was retrained with a second output channel predicting the spine (5-px union polylines; loss = mask Tversky+BCE + 0.5 x spine Tversky+BCE), with the checkpoint-selection runs' seed and data order and EMA weights, and only the mask channel kept at inference (`training/filament_spine_stagea.py`; fail-fast checks, per-epoch checkpoints, stop on error). Paired against the baseline EMA models on the same 557 out-of-fold images: with the refiner (the pre-set primary metric) 0.4015 vs 0.3998, +0.0016 (CI95 -0.004 to +0.007; per fold -0.009, -0.008, +0.004, -0.001, +0.023) -- not actionable; without the refiner 0.3713 vs 0.3639, +0.0074 (CI95 +0.002 to +0.013; TP +84, FP -57, FN -84). The spine signal genuinely improves stage-A's own detection -- the first training change in this study to do so significantly -- but the refiner already recovers most of the same filaments (after it, TP +20 only), so the gains overlap. Script `analysis/scripts/analyze_spine.py`, results `analysis/results/spine/`.

**Training-data diversity: the patch sampler repeats the same 1,760 patches every epoch (found 30 Sep 2026, not yet tested).** In the production stage-A training (`training/filament_unet_ensemble.py`, `PatchDS`) each patch is a deterministic function of its sample index only (`default_rng((i * 9781 + 12345) % 2**31)`), and the `DataLoader` does not shuffle. Run on the production class with synthetic images: the batches of epochs 1, 2 and 3 are identical, so the 30 epochs x 220 steps x batch 8 = 52,800 patch draws cover only 1,760 distinct patches (same crop position, same flips and rotation), taken from about 526 images per fold. Every stage-A model in this report (released, checkpoint-selection, consensus and spine runs) was trained this way, so the paired comparisons above stay fair, but all share the limitation. It is consistent with two earlier observations -- a training loss that falls to about 0.15 while fold-validation PQ swings between checkpoints (which made the production checkpoint rule so noisy), and stage-A gains that the refiner mostly absorbs -- but it has not been tested: the planned control is the same recipe with the patch seed also depending on the epoch (30 times more distinct patches at unchanged cost).

**Prediction morphology (qualitative).** `report/figures/fig_morph_overview.png` shows three full hold-out disks and `fig_morph_gallery.png` six examples per error type, for the final pipeline (released ensemble, near-limb filter applied), against the annotator pass with the most instances (`analysis/scripts/filament_morphology_figs_cpu.py`, CPU, stored predictions only). Counts on the 50 hold-out images against that pass: 258 matched, 68 near-misses (0.25 < IoU <= 0.5), 41 phantom predictions (touching no annotation), 93 missed filaments, 17 filaments split across two or more predictions; the near-limb filter removed 30 predictions, 24 of them false positives and 6 true positives. What the examples show: long, curved filaments are traced well along their length; near-misses are mostly extent disagreements -- the prediction covers one segment of a filament whose annotation continues through a faint section, or the annotation is broader than the dark core; missed filaments are mostly short, compact and low-contrast; many phantom predictions are dark elongated structures that look filament-like (the forum reports unannotated filaments); fragmentation happens where a long filament fades in the middle; the near-limb removals are mostly low-contrast dark patches close to the limb, while the lost true positives are genuine filaments near the limb.

**Ideas evaluated analytically and not run.** Because the hold-out cannot resolve differences below about 0.005 and GPU time
is limited, four candidate experiments were audited before any compute was spent. The estimates below are our own
judgements from the evidence available, not measurements.
* *Full clDice loss for the refiner* (precision and recall of soft skeletons). The closely related skeleton-recall loss was
  neutral to negative (-0.0037), the loss adds no signal against false positives (the largest loss), and the literature
  we found reports topology metrics for it, not instance-level PQ. Estimated effect -0.004 to +0.003.
* *Sato vesselness as an input channel* (precomputed for all images). We have no measurement that it separates filaments from
  other dark structures such as sunspots, the refiner already uses all three pretrained input channels, and it does not
  address false positives. Estimated effect -0.005 to +0.008.
* *A second, differently trained U-Net ensemble.* A second ensemble and an R34+R50 mix were already within +-0.002 once TTA is
  applied. Estimated effect 0 to +0.002.
* *Self-supervised pretraining on public unlabelled GONG H-alpha images.* The organizers explicitly permit it as long as it stays
  limited to training and does not leak into inference (forum topics 737215 and 744047; topic 738951, cited here earlier, is
  about using the original GONG FITS files and is not a permission for unlabelled pretraining), but we found no published evidence that it beats ImageNet initialisation for H-alpha filaments, the
  public solar foundation models we found were pretrained on SDO images rather than H-alpha, and the compute (roughly 3-6
  GPU-hours plus fine-tuning) exceeds our budget. We judge the chance of a gain of 0.01 or more to be below 20%.

## 5. Where the remaining error comes from
**Error decomposition** (final pipeline on the hold-out, TP/FP/FN = 400/239/211): 54% of the 450 errors are near-misses, i.e. a prediction and a ground-truth filament overlap but with IoU <= 0.5 (132 of 239 FP, 110 of 211 FN); the rest have no overlap at all. The mean IoU of true positives is 0.676.

**Annotator agreement.** Using one pass as the prediction and another as ground truth with the same rules (296 multi-pass images, 1,196 ordered pairs): PQ 0.340 (SE 0.008), mean matched IoU 0.634, and only 0.14% of matched pairs reach IoU 0.85. Near-misses make up 62% of the disagreement errors. Our pipeline exceeds this pairwise agreement (0.4325, IoU 0.676), plausibly because it predicts an average of several annotators; boundary quality beyond that is largely unresolvable from the labels. It is an indication of a ceiling, not a strict bound: a consensus of many annotators could do better than one.

**Training target.** A leave-one-pass-out simulation on 151 three-pass images builds predictions from two passes and scores them against the third. Predicting only objects that both passes marked, with the union shape, beats the pixel-union target (the one stage-A is trained on) by +0.0145 (paired bootstrap 95% CI +0.006 to +0.023); intersection shapes lose. Objects marked by one annotator only match a third pass about 22% of the time, below the break-even fraction of roughly 0.32 that follows from the PQ formula. This is a simulation of the target, not a trained model; because the tuned thresholds already suppress low-confidence objects, we expect most of this effect to be captured already and did not retrain.

**Sensitivity to the mask boundary** (`analysis/scripts/filament_label_probe_cpu.py`, 1 October). The pipeline's masks sit at a sharp optimum with respect to the training annotations: growing every instance by 1 px per side lowers the out-of-fold PQ from 0.3944 to 0.3554 (-0.039, CI95 -0.043 to -0.035), shrinking by 1 px to 0.3394 (-0.055, CI95 -0.061 to -0.050); 2 px costs -0.127 and -0.223 (hold-out: -0.059 / -0.049 at 1 px). The loss is mostly true positives falling below IoU 0.5 (4,487 to 4,198 and 4,035), not matched IoU. Two consequences: (i) a systematic boundary offset of about 1 px between the test and the training labels would by itself cost 0.04-0.05, the size of the hold-out-to-leaderboard gap; (ii) a leaderboard probe with masks grown or shrunk by 1 px is decisive: if the test labels follow the training convention it must read about 0.31-0.33, clearly below our 0.36. Removing small instances is not a free lunch either: dropping those below 700 px (26% of instances) costs -0.029. Both 1 px probes were submitted as diagnostics on 1 October: the dilated masks read 0.31 (submission 56751550; expected 0.31-0.33 if the test labels follow the training convention) and the eroded masks 0.32 (56751767; expected about 0.32). Both match the null prediction, so the test labels are neither systematically wider nor narrower than the training labels: our masks sit at the boundary optimum on the test set too, and a boundary-convention difference does not explain the hold-out-to-leaderboard gap. The probes also confirm that local PQ differences of this size carry over to the leaderboard at the expected rate.

**Learned instance scorer** (`analysis/scripts/filament_oof_instances_cpu.py`, `analyze_instance_scorer.py`, 1 October). Under PQ, keeping an instance adds its matched IoU to the numerator and 0.5 to the denominator in every pass, so it pays only if its expected matched IoU exceeds PQ/2. 33.6% of the 4,694 out-of-fold instances are a true positive in no pass; removing exactly the instances below the break-even would add +0.074 (oracle). A gradient-boosted regressor on 36 inference-time features (stage-A probability statistics, shape, local contrast, disk position, neighbourhood), cross-validated by image, reaches AUC 0.784 and, with the threshold fixed in advance at PQ/2 = 0.197 (no tuning), raises the out-of-fold PQ to 0.4088 (+0.0144, CI95 +0.011 to +0.018; 737 instances dropped, FP 3,231 to 2,225 at a cost of 218 TP). Fitted on all out-of-fold instances and applied to the hold-out ensemble's instances it gives 0.4383 to 0.4427 (+0.0043, CI95 -0.008 to +0.016; AUC 0.725): not significant on 50 images, but it passes the pre-set gate (no loss). Earlier filters built on about 420 hold-out candidates had failed (section 4); the difference is the training-set size. On the test set the scorer removes 184 of the 1,267 predicted instances (14.5%, against 15.7% out of fold and 13% on the hold-out; the CPU re-run that produced the features reproduced the earlier submission's masks on 180/180 images). Submitted on 1 October (submission 56759057), it reads 0.37 on the public leaderboard, up from 0.36: rank 167 of 806, about the middle of the 55-team 0.37 band, i.e. a hidden score near 0.370 against an estimated 0.363 before -- between the hold-out (+0.004) and the out-of-fold (+0.014) estimates after the usual 0.83 transfer. The scorer is the last stage of the released pipeline (`filament_scorer.py`, model in `models/`); the code path reproduces the submitted file byte for byte, and on Kaggle (2 October; LightGBM 4.6.0, torch 2.11, test suite passing) `Pipeline.segment` with the scorer reproduced the per-image official counts of all 50 hold-out images (pooled PQ 0.4427) and the submitted rows of four test images exactly.

**Looser candidates with the scorer: not adopted** (`analysis/scripts/filament_loose_candidates_cpu.py`, `analyze_loose_candidates.py`, 2 October). The candidate thresholds were tuned when nothing downstream could remove false positives, so we tested whether the scorer makes recall cheaper: candidates were re-extracted from the stored probability maps at looser thresholds, redrawn by the refiner, and the scorer was refitted per configuration (grouped CV, tau fixed at 0.1972). With p > 0.3, area >= 200, mean p >= 0.6 the unscored PQ falls (0.3943 to 0.3889 out of fold: +286 TP, +842 FP) and the scorer recovers more than that, 0.4115 against 0.4075 for the released thresholds with scorer: +0.0040 (CI95 +0.0001 to +0.0080), below the pre-set +0.005 margin, and -0.0028 on the hold-out (CI95 -0.017 to +0.011). Loosening further (p > 0.2, area >= 150, mean p >= 0.45) is worse: -0.0070 out of fold (CI95 -0.013 to -0.001), -0.0147 on the hold-out. The extra candidates are mostly unrecoverable false positives; the scorer's AUC rises only from 0.786 to 0.801. (Re-extracting the released configuration from the 8-bit stored maps changes the label map on 221 of 707 images and the unscored PQ by -0.0001 out of fold and -0.0014 on the hold-out; all comparisons above are within this re-extracted set.)

**Refiner-confidence and mask-stability features for the scorer: not adopted** (`analysis/scripts/filament_refiner_feats_cpu.py`, `analyze_scorer_refiner_feats.py`, 2 October). Eleven features per instance were added: the refiner probability inside the mask (mean, 10th percentile, maximum), the width of its uncertain band, and the IoU of the mask with the masks the refiner draws from a mirrored crop and from a smaller or larger seed box. The re-run refiner reproduced the stored mask of all 5,084 instances. The features are used by the model (the minimum stability IoU becomes the top feature by gain, 10%) but carry almost no new information: AUC 0.784 to 0.789 out of fold (0.725 to 0.732 on the hold-out), and the PQ does not improve: -0.0005 out of fold (CI95 -0.0025 to +0.0015), -0.0025 on the hold-out (CI95 -0.009 to +0.004). What separates the remaining false positives from true positives is therefore not visible to the refiner either; together with the loose-candidate result this points at the stage-A model (or at annotator disagreement) rather than at post-processing.

**Per-instance mask-variant selection: not adopted** (`analysis/scripts/filament_variants_cpu.py`, `analyze_mask_variants.py`, 3 October). Half of the remaining error is near-misses, so every instance of the released predictions (5,084 over the 657 out-of-fold and 50 hold-out images) was redrawn in ten ways: refiner thresholds 0.3/0.4/0.6/0.7, a mirrored crop, seed boxes padded 20% and 60%, the mean of four refiner passes, and the unrefined stage-A candidate, with the IoU against every annotator pass stored for each. The released mask was reproduced on all 5,084 instances, and the recomputed counts match the official ones on 653 of 657 images (pooled PQ 0.3943 against 0.3944). No single variant helps: switching every instance to one variant changes the out-of-fold PQ by between -0.021 (candidate) and +0.0012 (mean of four passes). An oracle that picks the best variant per instance by its true IoU gains +0.0223 (68% of instances would change variant; the oracle selects on the ground truth among ten noisy draws, so this is an upper bound, not an achievable value). A LightGBM selector predicting each variant's expected matched IoU from the scorer's features plus variant geometry, with the scorer's drop rule, does not capture any of it: -0.0013 out of fold (CI95 -0.0030 to +0.0003) and -0.0047 on the hold-out (CI95 -0.0096 to -0.0007) against the released variant with its scorer. Switching variants alone (keeping the reference's drops) is -0.0005. The variant that would have been better is not predictable from what the models can see, which closes the mask-shape line: neither more refiner capacity (generation 2), nor per-image or per-instance choices among refiner outputs, nor mask-stability features change the score.

**Multi-scale test-time augmentation: first positive stage-A result** (`analysis/scripts/filament_msc_tta_gpu.py`, `analyze_msc_tta.py`, 3 October). Stage-A averages 4 flips at one scale; the five fold models were re-scored on their own validation images (all 657, inference only, one T4, about 10.5 s per image, 114 minutes in two kernels) with scales 0.75 and 1.25 added, using the production candidate rule, refiner and near-limb filter, and paired against the single-scale baseline recomputed in the same run (which reproduces the stored CPU out-of-fold counts on 657/657 images). The three-scale average, fixed beforehand as the primary comparison, gains **+0.0117 PQ** (0.3944 to 0.4061; paired bootstrap CI95 +0.0067 to +0.0169; without the limb filter +0.0154, CI95 +0.0102 to +0.0206): false positives fall from 3,231 to 2,502 for a loss of 140 true positives. The other combinations (secondary, context only): 0.75 alone -0.0099, 1.25 alone -0.0122, 1.0+0.75 +0.0077, 1.0+1.25 +0.0049; averaging over scales helps, no single extra scale does. The first three folds alone (395 images) had given +0.0137, so the estimate shrank slightly on the last two. Caveats: (i) averaging shifts the probability calibration, so the instance scorer, fitted on single-scale features, has not seen these maps, and the gain over the released pipeline WITH its scorer (+0.0144) may be smaller since both remove false positives; (ii) the maps are from single fold models, not the released 5-model ensemble, which already averages 20 predictions at one scale.

**Multi-scale TTA on the released ensemble: hold-out gate failed, test run not made** (`analysis/scripts/filament_msc_ens_gpu.py`, `analyze_msc_ens_holdout.py`, 3 October). The ensemble (5 models x 4 flips) was run at one scale and at scales 0.75/1.0/1.25 on the 50 hold-out images in the same kernel (29 minutes on a T4; the one-scale arm reproduced the released ensemble's counts on 50/50 images). The three-scale ensemble scores 0.4269 against 0.4383 (-0.0114, paired CI95 -0.0304 to +0.0049; without the near-limb filter 0.4238 against 0.4325): false positives fall from 209 to 162 but true positives from 394 to 356. The rule stated before the run was that the three-scale ensemble must not fall below the one-scale ensemble, so the test set was not run. With 50 images the paired standard error is about 0.01: the hold-out neither confirms nor excludes a small gain, but it gives no support for the +0.0117 measured with single models, and the pattern (recall lost, thresholds tuned for one-scale ensemble maps) suggests that averaging over scales mostly removes variance that the 20-prediction ensemble has already removed. Recovering a gain would need the candidate thresholds re-tuned on multi-scale ensemble maps over many images, which only single-model out-of-fold maps (not the ensemble) allow; we judged that not worth the GPU quota.

**Test-versus-training shift (A1 of the handoff): the out-of-fold/leaderboard gap is not explained by observable differences** (`analysis/scripts/analyze_shift_a1.py`, `analysis/results/shift_a1_summary.json`, 14 October; criteria committed before the run). Only stored numbers were used (38 instance features of 4,694 out-of-fold, 390 hold-out and the 180 test images' predicted instances). A cross-validated (by image) classifier separating test from out-of-fold instances on the 26 geometry and image features reaches AUC 0.509, inside the range of 20 label permutations (0.440-0.557): no shift visible to the pipeline. With the probability features included the AUC is 0.760, but that comparison is confounded by design (out-of-fold images are predicted by one fold model, test images by the 5-model ensemble; the top features are mean, maximum and median probability); against the hold-out, which is ensemble-predicted too, the AUC is 0.40 (50 images, no power). Weighting the out-of-fold images to the test mixture (year, site, limb distance, contrast, brightness, mean area; effective n 591 of 657) moves the pooled PQ from 0.4088 to 0.4142 (CI95 0.4045 to 0.4237), i.e. away from the leaderboard value, not toward it; the scorer's predicted matched IoU is 0.391 on test against 0.404 on the hold-out and 0.395 out of fold, with 85%, 87% and 84% of instances kept. The test set has 14% of its images from 2019 or later against 9% out of fold, and instances 3% larger (median 1,228 against 1,190 px), neither of which changes the PQ estimate. What this cannot see is anything that does not change the predicted instances: the annotators' style or number of passes in the test labels, and the sampling noise of a public score computed on an unknown part of the 180 images. The leaderboard (0.37) should therefore be read as a noisy, level-shifted estimate, and decisions kept on the out-of-fold evidence.

**Per-epoch patch re-sampling of the stage-A training: not adopted, clearly worse** (`training/filament_resample_stagea.py`, `analysis/scripts/analyze_resample.py`, `analysis/results/resample/`, 14 October; rule fixed beforehand). The production sampler draws each patch from a generator seeded by the sample index only and the loader does not shuffle, so the 30 epochs of the released recipe see 1,760 distinct patches (52,800 draws). The five fold models were retrained with exactly one change, new patches in every epoch (same crop policy, augmentation, steps, seed, initialisation, folds, EMA; 2.7 hours on a T4 for the five folds), and scored on the same 557 out-of-fold images as the checkpoint-selection baseline, with the production thresholds and the released refiner. The rule was a paired gain of at least +0.005 with CI95 above 0. The result is the opposite: EMA weights 0.3998 -> 0.3796 (-0.0202, CI95 -0.0269 to -0.0136), last-epoch weights 0.3977 -> 0.3783 (-0.0194), without the refiner 0.3639 -> 0.3302 (-0.0337); every fold is lower (0.410->0.387, 0.406->0.371, 0.391->0.377, 0.396->0.373, 0.398->0.392). True positives rise (3,934 -> 4,166) but false positives rise far more (2,909 -> 4,121). The retrained models are therefore more confident outside the filaments, and the fixed candidate rule (probability 0.4, mean probability 0.7, area), the refiner and the scorer, all fitted to the maps of the models trained on the repeated patches, are not suited to them. We then tested whether recalibration recovers the loss (`analysis/scripts/analyze_recal.py`, `analysis/results/recal_summary.json`; rule fixed beforehand, same bar; the maps were recomputed on CPU). Both model sets were scored on the same 557 images with the near-limb filter, and the instance scorer was refit on each set's own instances (5-fold cross-validation by image, tau = half of that set's near-limb PQ). Without the scorer the retrained models remain lower (0.3909 against 0.4058, -0.0149, CI95 -0.0217 to -0.0078). After the refit the gap nearly closes, 0.4140 against 0.4177 (-0.0037, CI95 -0.0101 to +0.0024): the refit scorer removes most of the extra false positives (3,394 -> 1,990), but the result is not a gain. Re-sampling is not adopted and the released models stay.

**Rubric metrics other than PQ: Dice, IoU/Dice distributions, one-to-many and many-to-one** (`analysis/scripts/filament_rubric_cpu.py`, `analyze_rubric.py`, `analysis/results/rubric_summary.json`, 14 October). The final evaluation lists, besides PQ, the mean Dice score, the distributions of Dice and IoU and the distribution of one-to-many and many-to-one relations; the quantities were computed as in the organizers' self-evaluation notebook (pairwise IoU/Dice matrices per annotator-image, pairs with a score above 0 for the distributions, partners with IoU above 0 for the relations) from the stored released predictions (CPU kernel, no inference; the recomputed PQ counts equal the official ones on every image). The configuration of the mean-Dice scorer (`torchmetrics DiceScore`) is not stated; we report the foreground Dice of the union masks per image, and the macro (foreground+background) and micro (pixel accuracy) variants for reference. Released pipeline (near-limb filter + instance scorer): out-of-fold (657 images) PQ 0.4088, mean Dice 0.624 per image (0.630 per annotator-image; macro 0.815, micro 0.998); hold-out (50 images) PQ 0.4427, mean Dice 0.664 (CI95 0.618-0.699). Pairwise IoU over overlapping pairs: mean 0.578, 73% above 0.5; Dice: mean 0.712. Relations: of 7,588 ground-truth filaments 26.8% have no overlapping prediction, 69.8% exactly one, 3.4% two or more; of 6,478 predictions 12.3% overlap no ground truth, 85.3% exactly one, 2.4% two or more. Over-merging is therefore rare (2.4% of predictions touch two or more annotated filaments) and fragmentation is small (3.4% of annotated filaments are split); the dominant relations are the misses. **The instance scorer trades Dice for PQ**: without it (near-limb filter only) the out-of-fold per-image Dice is 0.6326, with it 0.6241 (paired difference -0.0084, CI95 -0.0141 to -0.0035; hold-out -0.0123, CI95 -0.0207 to -0.0048), because dropping 16% of the instances raises the share of unmatched annotated filaments from 20.6% to 26.8%, while PQ rises 0.3944 to 0.4088. A sweep of the scorer threshold on the out-of-fold images shows that the Dice loss is concentrated between thresholds 0.15 and 0.25 (PQ / Dice at 0.10: 0.4022 / 0.6320; 0.15: 0.4063 / 0.6313; 0.197: 0.4088 / 0.6241; 0.25: 0.4069 / 0.6123). The relative weights of Dice and PQ in the rubric are not published, so the threshold that suits the final evaluation cannot be derived; the released threshold is the PQ-optimal one. These numbers are one-parameter selections on the same out-of-fold images and carry no held-out confirmation.

**Two prepared threshold configurations for the final submissions** (`InstanceScorer.load(tau=...)`, `analysis/scripts/make_scorer_submission.py <dir> [tau]`, `analysis/results/rubric_summary.json`, 14 October). The scorer threshold is the only difference. *Released* (tau 0.1972, the out-of-fold PQ / 2): out-of-fold PQ 0.4089 and per-image Dice 0.6241, hold-out 0.4427 and 0.6635, 84% (out-of-fold) and 87% (hold-out) of the instances kept; on the test set 1,083 of 1,267 instances are submitted. *Balanced* (tau 0.15): out-of-fold PQ 0.4063 and Dice 0.6313, hold-out 0.4418 and 0.6681, 90% / 92% kept; 1,143 of 1,267 test instances. Paired, balanced minus released: out-of-fold PQ -0.0027 (CI95 -0.0046 to -0.0007) and Dice +0.0072 (CI95 +0.0040 to +0.0112); hold-out PQ -0.0009 (CI95 -0.0089 to +0.0063) and Dice +0.0046 (CI95 +0.0013 to +0.0084). The balanced configuration therefore gives up a small, out-of-fold-significant amount of PQ for a larger Dice gain; with equal weights it is ahead by about +0.0045 (out-of-fold) in the sum, but the rubric weights are not published and 0.15 was selected on the same out-of-fold images (one parameter, no independent confirmation). Both test files validate against the submission format (176 of 180 images with predictions, as in the released submission); neither has been submitted.

## 6. Discussion and limitations
* Hold-out resolution (SE 0.017) means several of our null results are inconclusive rather than negative. The "noise floor" of 0.0016 that our earlier experiments used as a submission gate came from a single quantisation difference (uint8 versus float32 probability maps) and is roughly ten times smaller than the sampling error, so decisions made with it are provisional; reducing the error would need out-of-fold predictions over the 657 training images (standard error about 0.004) or repeated seeds.
* **Hold-out versus leaderboard.** The leaderboard score (0.36) is below the hold-out PQ (0.4325). We do not have the test labels and did not identify the cause, so the following are hypotheses only: (i) the hold-out is a random subset of the training images and the test images may be distributed differently (for example by observation date); (ii) if the test ground truth comes from fewer annotator passes per image than our multi-pass average, the attainable PQ is lower, since two annotators agree with each other at only 0.34 -- **not supported by the out-of-fold evaluation (section 3)**: on 657 images the model scores the same on single-pass and multi-pass images (difference +0.010, CI95 -0.007 to +0.028); the -0.063 single-pass deficit seen on the 50-image hold-out was sampling noise; (iii) 50 images give a standard error of 0.017, and the thresholds were tuned on them -- consistent with this, a single stage-A model scores 0.388 out of fold, and 131-image folds range from 0.372 to 0.407, so the hold-out's 0.4325 is plausibly on the optimistic side (part of the gap is also single model versus 5-model ensemble, which we have not separated). What does hold is that the ordering of our own submissions agreed with the hold-out ordering: 0.3754 (proxy) scored 0.33, an R34+R50 mix (0.4030, proxy) scored 0.34, and the refiner pipeline (0.4325) scored 0.36.
* Roughly 58% of training images have one annotator pass and give no consensus signal; a consensus-target retraining would be limited to the multi-pass subset.
* **A stronger stage-A exists publicly (measured, not used in our submission).** Another participant's public weights (Kaggle dataset dariushafshar/solar-filament-unet-weights, CC BY 4.0: U-Net ResNet34 + U-Net EfficientNetV2-S per fold, 10,500 steps, soft multi-annotator target) scored out of fold on all 707 training images with their folds (two models per image, 4 test-time views): their keep rule gives PQ 0.4484 (their card: 0.4502 with 8 views); adding our near-limb filter and our instance scorer refit on those maps gives 0.4552 (+0.0068, CI95 +0.0035 to +0.0104), so the scorer transfers to maps it was not designed on. On the 657 images where our released pipeline has out-of-fold predictions it scores 0.4546 against our 0.4089 (+0.0457, CI95 +0.0388 to +0.0526; theirs averages two models per image, ours one). The comparison rules were fixed before the data existed (analysis/scripts/analyze_pubw.py). The stage-A map is therefore our limiting component, and by a margin about seven times larger than what our post-processing adds.
* We did not use, and the pipeline does not depend on, any labels outside the competition annotations.

## 7. Leaderboard context
We report where the pipeline stands rather than optimise against the upper end of the leaderboard, because that end is
not consistent with legitimate modelling quality. Descriptive facts from the public leaderboard (657 teams, downloaded on
20 September 2026; scores rounded to two decimals): the scores form a smooth, bell-shaped distribution that ends at 0.41
(2 teams at 0.41, 9 at 0.40); there is then no team between 0.42 and 0.48 and one at 0.49, followed by 41 teams between
0.545 and 0.555 and five teams at 0.56-0.62. Our 0.36 ranks about 143rd of 657, or about 96th of the roughly 610 teams
below that gap. This is context, not a statement about any particular team. Among the public notebooks we inspected, one trains a small network but does not use it and instead writes out a pre-computed submission embedded in the notebook (1,342 masks over 178 test images); its last run (3 September) is later than the last-submission dates of several teams in the 0.55 group (from 26 August), so it cannot be the only source of that group. We did not use its content.

The organizers have stated that scores of this kind are not considered valid ("we can easily weed out the fake ones",
competition forum topic 740400), that any PQ above 0.35 is "of great value" to them (competition announcement of
20 August), and that ground-truth annotations beyond those provided by the competition must not be used, otherwise
submissions are disregarded (topic 729396). Participants report in the forum that a public release of MAGFiLO contains
annotations for the competition's test images (topics 728062, 737177, 741892); we could not and did not verify this.
The organizers also state that their evaluation "is not based on the scores shown in the leaderboard", which is only
"a preliminary way of filtering some submissions out" (topic 728062), and that the final evaluation combines quantitative
and qualitative criteria (competition "Evaluation" page).

## 8. Compliance and data provenance
* **Training data.** Only the competition's own training images and annotations were used. Every model comparison uses a
  50-image hold-out drawn from the training images.
* **Public MAGFiLO release.** MAGFiLO [9] is also released publicly (Harvard Dataverse, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). We did not
  download or consult that release or any dataset derived from it, and we used no test-image labels, dumped predictions or
  third-party submission files for training, tuning, model selection or evaluation.
* **Disclosure.** To understand the upper part of the leaderboard we decoded one public Kaggle notebook that embeds a
  pre-computed submission and computed only descriptive statistics of it (instance count, areas, overlaps). We never
  compared our predictions with it, and it played no role in any training, tuning, selection or evaluation decision.
* **External models.** ImageNet-pretrained ResNet34 encoders initialise the networks. A public third-party Mask R-CNN
  model (Kaggle Model `phuongncn/solar-filament-i079-weights`, Apache-2.0) was evaluated once as an ablation on our
  hold-out and is excluded from the released pipeline because its training data cannot be verified.
* **Inference.** Predictions use only the H-alpha test images.
* **AI assistance.** The code, analyses and this document were developed with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code) under
  the author's direction.

## 9. Conclusions and outlook
* **What is robust.** A two-stage pipeline (5-fold U-Net ensemble with flip TTA plus a crop refiner) reaches hold-out PQ 0.4325
  and leaderboard 0.36. The refiner (+0.0177) is the only change with a measurable effect; everything else we tried was
  indistinguishable from zero at the resolution of a 50-image hold-out.
* **What limits it.** Annotators agree with each other at only PQ 0.34, most remaining errors are boundary near-misses
  or disagreements about which objects exist, and on the multi-pass hold-out images about half (54%) of the false-positive counts come
  from candidates that at least one other annotator marked.
* **What has some support.** Predicting only objects marked by at least two passes is worth about +0.0145 PQ in a
  target simulation, but a classifier for that property gained only +0.0024 out of sample (oracle bound +0.0132), so any
  realised gain is small.
* **What we would do with more time or compute.** First estimate the noise properly (out-of-fold predictions and repeated
  seeds), then test a consensus-target retraining on the multi-pass subset (about 42% of the training images); pursue
  domain-specific pretraining only if a cheap CPU test shows a domain shift between ImageNet and H-alpha features.
* **What we deliberately did not do.** Optimise against the upper end of the leaderboard or use any annotations beyond the
  competition's own (see sections 7 and 8).

## 10. Reproducibility
Code, pinned requirements and an end-to-end notebook are in the repository; the notebook was validated on Kaggle from the weights (identical per-image official counts on 3 hold-out images; format-valid output on 2 test images). The full 180-image submission was produced by the same code path in earlier kernels. Repository: https://github.com/Alphine/filament (default branch `final`).
